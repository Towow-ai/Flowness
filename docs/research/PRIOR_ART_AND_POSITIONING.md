# Prior Art and Positioning

Flowness is intentionally positioned beside mature work rather than by pretending adjacent ideas do not exist.

## Harness engineering

Modern harness work correctly shifts engineering effort from prompt wording to the environment, tools, context, constraints, tests, and feedback around an Agent. Flowness depends on that move. Its additional hypothesis is that a single harnessed execution is not the right persistent unit for long-running engineering work.

## Long-running agents and issue-centered orchestration

Progress files, issue trackers, external state, and restartable sessions already establish that work can outlive a context window or worker. Flowness must not claim this property as unique. It proposes a stronger semantic surface around persistent Work: exact state, current obligations, concept and evidence versions, unresolved findings, graph projections, invalidation, effect, activation, acceptance, and human-governed promotion.

## Graph runtimes

Graph runtimes provide nodes, edges, shared state, dynamic transitions, pause/resume, checkpoints, and human interrupts. Flowness does not claim graph orchestration as novel. Its positioning is that a graph is a versioned projection of the current Flow, and changed facts may invalidate a graph and require the future execution structure to be recompiled.

## Durable workflow engines

Temporal, Prefect, and related systems are strong baselines for reliable long-running execution, retries, scheduling, observability, and worker failure. Flowness should integrate mature durability infrastructure where possible. Its proposed additional layer is semantic Work state, current-world context compilation, cognitive-layer reflow, human constitutional boundaries, and reality-bound closure.

## Agent software-engineering platforms

OpenHands and other coding-Agent systems provide sandboxes, tools, planning, runtime execution, and benchmark integration. Flowness is not positioned as a general replacement for every coding Agent. It is an opinionated runtime and mechanism kit for teams that need work to remain coherent across many attempts, interpretations, roles, and acceptance boundaries.

## Knowledge graphs, ADRs, specifications, and documentation

Concept graphs, ADRs, RFCs, schemas, and architecture notations preserve important engineering meaning. Flowness treats selected interpretation and decision history as runtime-consumable, versioned state rather than only documentation. This does not make existing notations obsolete; it creates an integration problem between human-legible design artifacts and machine-consumable lifecycle state.

## Distributed systems

Event sourcing, CQRS, snapshots, commit protocols, version chains, and sagas provide mature concepts for causal history and consistency. Flowness’s technical-report line asks what changes when writers are non-deterministic, can mutate interpretation, and consume only bounded local views. The formal claims of that line must remain separate from broader Flow Engineering product claims.

## Flow Engineering naming boundary

“Flow engineering” has prior uses in code-generation workflows, value-stream/organizational methods, and company names. Flowness therefore uses a recognition posture, not a first-use claim:

> We use **Flow Engineering** for the engineering of persistent Work moving through changing agentic execution structures.

The project’s contribution must be judged by the clarity, implementation, cases, and evidence of that definition—not by ownership of the phrase.
